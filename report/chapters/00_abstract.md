# 摘　　要

亚马逊棋（Game of the Amazons）规则简单，但每一步由“移动一个后”和“射出一支箭”两部分组成，开局一步就有 2176 种合法走法，是研究大分支因子博弈搜索的典型平台。本文以 10×10 标准亚马逊棋为对象，在统一的引擎与统一的领域知识（Lieberum 式领地评估、本文设计的“可分离评估”着法排序、分隔后的填格策略）之上，实现并比较了两类经典的人工智能方法：带迭代加深、主变例搜索、置换表、杀手与历史启发和束宽剪枝的 α-β 博弈树搜索[@knuth1975]，以及采用 UCT/PUCT 选择、渐进展开与评估截断模拟的蒙特卡洛树搜索（MCTS）[@kocsis2006; @browne2012]。此外还实现了两种轻量级学习方法：以自对弈棋谱训练的线性 softmax 走子策略（作为 MCTS 的先验），以及用 Texel 方法拟合的评估函数权重。所有算法都被打包进一个可直接在浏览器中运行的单文件网页游戏，引擎在 Web Worker 中运行，界面实时展示双方领地、候选着法、搜索统计、主变例与局势曲线。

实验部分共进行了 2144 局主实验对局（循环赛、α-β 消融、MCTS 消融、算力缩放与同预算对比）、384 局独立种子的重复实验，以及决策质量、搜索效率与随机性测试。主要结果如下：（1）在 200 ms/步的循环赛中，完整 α-β（+427 Elo，相对一步贪心）与 MCTS-PUCT（+362）处于同一档，含重复实验在内的 160 局直接对局中 α-β 得 60.0%；不使用领域知识的纯随机 MCTS 比一步贪心还弱 771 Elo。（2）α-β 中作用最大的是着法排序与评估函数，束宽加倍显著更强，而置换表、杀手启发与 PVS 没有显著影响。（3）MCTS 的强度主要来自先验与短模拟：换成 UCT 选择损失约 390 Elo，随机模拟到终局几乎全负。（4）两类方法的强弱随时间预算翻转：50—100 ms 时 α-β 显著占优，800 ms 时 MCTS 显著占优。（5）学习得到的走子策略在离线指标上优于手工排序，但作为先验的棋力提升不稳定（5 批共 160 局合计得分 46.2%，与 50% 无显著差别）；Texel 权重降低了胜负预测的交叉熵，却让 α-β 变弱了约 290 Elo。失败案例分析中发现了第一版程序“分隔判定”的逻辑漏洞（漏掉敌对的后相邻的情形），本文修复后重跑了全部实验，并保留第一版数据，如实报告该漏洞对结果的影响。

本文没有提出新算法，贡献在于对已有方法的复现、在同一平台上的系统比较、可分离评估这一工程设计及决策过程的可视化。所有实验数据均来自真实运行，原始记录随代码一并提交；文中对 AI 工具的使用、未完成与无法核实的部分均作了说明。全部代码、数据与报告源文件公开于 https://github.com/NEUMelon/amazon-ai ，网页游戏可在 https://neumelon.github.io/amazon-ai/ 在线试玩。

**关键词：**亚马逊棋；α-β 剪枝；蒙特卡洛树搜索；博弈树搜索；束宽剪枝；Texel 调参

# Abstract

The Game of the Amazons has simple rules, but every move combines a queen move and an arrow shot, giving 2176 legal moves in the opening position; this makes it a standard testbed for game-tree search with a very large branching factor. Using 10×10 Amazons and a single engine with shared domain knowledge (a Lieberum-style territory evaluation, a "separable evaluation" move ordering designed in this work, and a common filling policy after separation), this report implements and compares two classical families of methods: alpha-beta search with iterative deepening, principal variation search, a transposition table, killer and history heuristics and beam pruning; and Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) with UCT/PUCT selection, progressive widening and evaluation-cutoff playouts. Two lightweight learning methods are added: a linear softmax move policy trained on self-play games (used as the MCTS prior) and evaluation weights fitted with Texel tuning. Everything is packaged as a single-file browser game; the engine runs in a Web Worker, and the interface shows territory, candidate moves, search statistics, principal variations and a win-rate curve.

The evaluation comprises 2144 games (a round robin, alpha-beta and MCTS ablations, time scaling and equal-budget comparisons), 384 replication games with fresh seeds, and decision-quality, efficiency and variance tests. At 200 ms per move, full alpha-beta (+427 Elo relative to a one-ply greedy player) and PUCT-MCTS (+362) are in the same tier, while MCTS with pure random playouts is 771 Elo below greedy. Move ordering and the evaluation function matter most for alpha-beta, and doubling the beam width is significantly stronger, whereas the transposition table, killer moves and PVS show no significant effect. MCTS relies on its prior and on short playouts. The ranking flips with the time budget: alpha-beta is significantly stronger at 50–100 ms and MCTS at 800 ms. The learned policy beats hand-made ordering on offline metrics but its gain in play is unstable (46.2% over 160 games, not significantly different from 50%), and the Texel weights predict outcomes better yet make alpha-beta about 290 Elo weaker. Failure analysis revealed a logic bug in the first version's separation test (adjacent enemy queens were missed); it was fixed, all experiments were rerun, and the first-version data are kept to report the bug's impact. No new algorithm is claimed; all data come from real runs and are released with the code at https://github.com/NEUMelon/amazon-ai (playable online at https://neumelon.github.io/amazon-ai/).

**Keywords:** Game of the Amazons; alpha-beta pruning; Monte Carlo tree search; game-tree search; beam search; Texel tuning
